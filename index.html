Understanding The Threat

Not Feeling Better Quickly Despite Taking Medicines?

The reason could be Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR). Understand the key facts through these 3 essential campaign questions:

1

What is AMR?

When antibiotics lose their effectiveness against germs and bacteria, that condition is called Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR).

🧫 Germs ➔ 💊 Antibiotics ➔ 🦠 Resistant Germs

Over time, resistant germs multiply and standard medicines stop working completely.

2

Why is AMR Dangerous?

  • 💊
    Medicines Become Ineffective: Standard drugs fail to cure common infections.
  • ⏳
    Prolonged Illness: Infections take much longer to recover.
  • 🏥
    Complex Treatments: Medical care & procedures become high-risk.
  • 💰
    Rising Costs: Treatment & hospital expenses increase.
  • 🚨
    Severe Risk: Risk of severe complications & mortality rises.
3

What Can We Do to Prevent AMR?

Three things: take antibiotics only when a doctor prescribes them, finish every course, and never bin or flush what is left over.

Why It Happens

How Resistance Happens

The same mistake creates resistant bacteria and leaves antibiotics sitting in your cupboard. Here is how, step by step.

Inside the body, over one course of antibiotics

  1. 1

    You fall ill

    An infection takes hold and the bacteria multiply quickly inside the body.

  2. 2

    The doctor prescribes a course

    Say five days. The dose and the length are chosen to kill all of the bacteria, not most of them.

  3. 3

    Day three: you feel better

    The weakest bacteria die first. That is why the symptoms fade well before the infection is actually gone.

  4. 4

    You stop taking it

    The toughest bacteria are still alive — the very ones the antibiotic struggled with most. And the rest of the strip stays in the cupboard.

  5. 5

    The survivors multiply

    With nothing left to stop them, the bacteria that shrugged off the antibiotic take over.

  6. 6

    Next time, the medicine fails

    For you, and for anyone you pass the infection to. That is antimicrobial resistance.

Two habits break this chain: finish every course your doctor prescribes, and never leave the leftovers lying around.

Safe Disposal

The Disposal Journey

Seven steps that take a leftover tablet from your shelf to safe destruction — and keep antibiotics working for everyone.

# योग्य विल्हेवाट, AMR वर मात!

Safe disposal, every single time

  1. 1

    Leftover medicines at home

    Unfinished or expired tablets, syrups and capsules are still sitting in the house.

  2. 2

    Never bin it or flush it

    Thrown in the bin or flushed away, medicines reach soil and water. Safe disposal exists to stop exactly that.

  3. 3

    Take them to your PHC

    Carry them to your nearest PHC, sub-centre or government health facility.

  4. 4

    Hand them over

    Give them to the staff, or put them in the medicine collection box if there is one.

  5. 5

    Staff collect and seal them

    Health staff take charge of what is handed in and store it safely until it is taken for disposal.

  6. 6

    Destroyed the safe way

    It is disposed of through the health system, not left in a landfill or a water body.

  7. 7

    Soil and water stay clean

    Antibiotics keep working for the next person who needs them — and the cycle starts again at home.

Do it today: take any leftover or expired medicines to your nearest PHC, sub-centre or government health facility, and ask the staff there.
